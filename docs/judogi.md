# Judogi

The jacket, the trousers and the belt as original SVG: contest white or blue, the rank belts, named sizes, laid out as separate pieces (as product photographs show them) or worn as a suit. Each piece is a garment with volume that turns, drawn by the same pipeline as the jerseys: the jacket's body is thick to the hem and its heavy sleeves hang from a dropped shoulder; the trousers' hips part into two legs, so they turn as two legs and not as one board. The designer keeps every choice in the address and saves SVG or PNG.

## What is drawn

- **Jacket (uwagi):** the collar standing round the back of the neck and running down the front as a band of six stitched rows, the wearer's left crossing over the right; the inside of the neck in shadow; a fine grain weave on the upper body and quilted diamonds on the skirt below the waist seam; side vents; turned and stitched cuffs and hem.
- **Trousers (zubon):** a deep waistband gathered by a drawstring tied in a bow at the front; the hips going over into two legs; a second layer over each knee, stitched round; a turned hem with a break above the foot.
- **Belt (obi):** many stitched rows, tied round the waist with a square knot and two ends hanging 20–30 cm; on its own it is drawn as a tied ring. Red-white is the panels of a kohaku belt.

No maker, federation or sponsor mark is drawn. Sizes and positions cite the [IJF Judogi Rules (09 03 2022)](https://78884ca60822a34fb0e6-082b8fd5551e97bc65e327988b444396.ssl.cf3.rackcdn.com/up/2022/04/IJF_Judogi_rules_-_09_03_2022_-1649423105.pdf) as facts. That document's pictures and wording are not copied.

```js
import { renderJudogi, judogiFrame, judogiCheck } from "@johnmorrisdotca/senshu-jerseys";

renderJudogi({ colour: "white", mode: "pieces", belt: "white", size: "170" });
judogiFrame({ colour: "blue", mode: "jacket", yaw: 40 });
judogiCheck({ size: "180" });
```

`mode` is `pieces` (the jacket with its belt tied, and the trousers beside it), `suit`, `jacket`, `pants` or `belt`; `view` is `front`, `back` or `flat` (the pieces laid out with no volume, the belt beside them).

`judogiCheck` reports whether a named size meets the cited length facts (jacket to mid-thigh, sleeves leaving about 5 cm to the wrist, trousers leaving about 5 cm to the ankle, belt 4–5 cm wide with 20–30 cm hanging ends). Traditional sizes 1–7 map onto those heights.

## On a page

A judogi is detailed cloth, and every one of the few hundred cells a piece is made of draws its face again: drawn as it is, a pair of pieces takes about a second to paint. On a page, `judogiPrepare` paints each face once into a picture before the drawing is shown, and the cells show the picture; then `judogiTurn` moves the drawing to another angle in place, at about thirty frames a second on a desk. The SVG the package returns, and so a saved file, keeps the drawing itself.

```js no-run
import { judogiFrame } from "@johnmorrisdotca/senshu-jerseys";
import { judogiPrepare, judogiTurn } from "@johnmorrisdotca/senshu-jerseys/turn";

const holder = document.createElement("div");
holder.innerHTML = judogiFrame({ mode: "pieces", belt: "black" });
const svg = holder.firstElementChild;
await judogiPrepare(svg);          // before it goes on the page
document.querySelector("#stage").append(svg);
judogiTurn(svg, 30);               // and again for each angle
```

The shade is opaque greys that multiply onto the cloth (CSS `mix-blend-mode`), which every current browser draws; a white cloth would show the grid of a half-transparent shade as lines.

Later tickets add back patches, embroidery and labels onto the same options object.
